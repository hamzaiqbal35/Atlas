The **Big Bounce** is a hypothesized cosmological model for the origin of the known universe. It proposes that the Big Bang was not an absolute singularity from which space and time were created out of nothing, but rather the rebound of a previous, collapsing universe.

## Avoiding the Singularity

In classical general relativity, the mathematics of the Big Bang inevitably lead to a singularity—a point of infinite density and temperature where the laws of physics break down. 

However, physicists widely believe that general relativity is incomplete because it does not incorporate quantum mechanics. Theories of quantum gravity suggest that space and time themselves may be quantized (made of discrete, indivisible units) at the Planck scale ($10^{-35}$ meters).

## Loop Quantum Cosmology (LQC)

One of the leading frameworks supporting a Big Bounce is **Loop Quantum Cosmology**, an application of Loop Quantum Gravity to the universe as a whole. 

In LQC, as a universe collapses and its density approaches the Planck density, the discrete nature of quantum geometry generates an intensely repulsive quantum force. This force acts as a brake on the collapse.

The modified Friedmann equation in LQC takes the form:

$$
H^2 = \frac{8\pi G}{3} \rho \left( 1 - \frac{\rho}{\rho_{\text{crit}}} \right)
$$

where $\rho_{\text{crit}}$ is the maximum allowable density (on the order of the Planck density). 

As the collapsing universe's density $\rho$ approaches $\rho_{\text{crit}}$, the term in the parentheses approaches zero, meaning the Hubble parameter $H$ (the expansion/contraction rate) goes to zero. The collapse halts completely. Because the repulsive force is enormous, the universe immediately rebounds, springing outward in a new "Big Bang."

## A Cyclic Cosmos

If the Big Bounce is correct, our universe might be just one iteration in an infinite sequence of expanding and contracting cosmoses—a cyclic universe. 

In such a model, time has no beginning and no end. However, these models must address the problem of entropy: if entropy increases in every cycle (as dictated by the Second Law of Thermodynamics), each subsequent universe would last longer and grow larger than the last, implying that tracing the cycles backward still requires a definitive beginning in the finite past.

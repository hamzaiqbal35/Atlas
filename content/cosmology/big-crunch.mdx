The **Big Crunch** is a hypothetical scenario for the ultimate fate of the universe in which the expansion of space eventually reverses, and the universe recollapses. This scenario dominated cosmological thought before the discovery of dark energy in 1998.

## The Density Parameter ($\Omega$)

The fate of a universe without dark energy is dictated entirely by a tug-of-war between the outward momentum of its expansion and the inward pull of its own gravity. This is parameterized by the density parameter $\Omega$, defined as the ratio of the actual density of the universe ($\rho$) to the critical density ($\rho_c$):

$$
\Omega = \frac{\rho}{\rho_c} \quad \text{where} \quad \rho_c = \frac{3H_0^2}{8\pi G}
$$

For a Big Crunch to occur, the universe must be "closed," meaning its density exceeds the critical density ($\Omega > 1$). In such a universe, the gravitational attraction of all the matter is strong enough to eventually halt the expansion and pull everything back together.

## The Reversal

In a closed universe, the expansion would gradually slow down. Galaxies would stop receding from one another. For a brief cosmic moment, the universe would be static. 

Then, the contraction would begin. At first, the blue-shift of galaxies rushing toward us would be the only indicator. Over billions of years, the contraction would accelerate. 

## The Collapse

As the universe shrinks, the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) would blueshift, vastly increasing in temperature. The sequence of cosmic history would play out in reverse, but with increasing violence:

1. **Galactic Collisions**: As space shrinks, galaxy clusters would merge into super-clusters, and individual galaxies would collide incessantly, disrupting stellar orbits and funneling matter into supermassive black holes.
2. **Boiling the Stars**: When the CMB temperature exceeds the surface temperature of stars (a few thousand Kelvin), stars would be unable to cool by radiating heat. They would boil from the outside in and eventually explode.
3. **Atomic Dissociation**: As the temperature reaches millions of degrees, atoms themselves would be ripped apart into a dense plasma, recreating the conditions of the primordial universe.
4. **The Final Singularity**: Ultimately, all matter and spacetime itself would be crushed into a singularity of infinite density and temperature—a reversal of the Big Bang.

## Current Perspectives

Observations of distant supernovae in the late 1990s revealed that the expansion of the universe is not slowing down, but **accelerating**, driven by a mysterious force called dark energy. Because dark energy currently dominates the energy density of the cosmos ($\Omega_\Lambda \approx 0.68$), the standard model strongly suggests the universe is flat ($\Omega = 1$) and will expand forever, making a Big Crunch highly unlikely.

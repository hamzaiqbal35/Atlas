Cosmic inflation is a theory of exponential expansion of space in the early universe. The inflationary epoch lasted from $10^{-36}$ seconds after the conjectured Big Bang singularity to some time between $10^{-33}$ and $10^{-32}$ seconds after the singularity. Following the inflationary period, the universe continued to expand, but at a much slower rate.

## The Horizon and Flatness Problems

Before the advent of inflation theory, the standard Big Bang model suffered from severe fine-tuning problems:

1. **The Horizon Problem**: Why is the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) so uniform ($\Delta T/T \sim 10^{-5}$) across opposite sides of the sky, when those regions were seemingly never in causal contact?
2. **The Flatness Problem**: Why is the universe's spatial geometry so incredibly close to flat ($\Omega_k \approx 0$), which requires the early universe's density to be fine-tuned to one part in $10^{62}$?

Inflation solves both by proposing an exponential expansion. A tiny, causally connected patch was blown up to become our entire observable universe (solving the horizon problem), and this extreme stretching flattened any initial curvature (solving the flatness problem).

## The Inflaton Field

Inflation is driven by a hypothetical scalar field $\phi$ called the **inflaton**. The dynamics of this field dictate the universe's expansion via the Friedmann equations. During inflation, the potential energy $V(\phi)$ of the field dominates its kinetic energy $\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^2$, leading to a negative pressure equation of state ($w \approx -1$).

The scale factor $a(t)$ evolves exponentially:
$$
a(t) = a(0) e^{Ht}
$$
where $H$ is the Hubble parameter, which remains nearly constant during the inflationary epoch.

## Slow-Roll Dynamics

For inflation to last long enough (at least 60 e-folds, $N \ge 60$) to solve the Big Bang's problems, the inflaton field must roll slowly down its potential energy curve. This is governed by the slow-roll parameters:

$$
\epsilon = \frac{M_{\text{Pl}}^2}{2} \left( \frac{V'(\phi)}{V(\phi)} \right)^2 \ll 1
$$
$$
\eta = M_{\text{Pl}}^2 \frac{V''(\phi)}{V(\phi)} \ll 1
$$

When the field finally reaches the minimum of its potential, it oscillates and decays into standard model particles, a process known as **reheating**, which initiates the radiation-dominated era of the standard Big Bang model.

## Quantum Fluctuations

Perhaps the most profound consequence of inflation is that microscopic quantum fluctuations in the inflaton field were stretched to macroscopic cosmic scales. These density perturbations seeded the formation of all large-scale structures (galaxies and galaxy clusters) we observe today.

## Overview
A gluon is an elementary particle that acts as the exchange particle (or gauge boson) for the strong force between quarks. It is analogous to the exchange of photons in the electromagnetic force between two charged particles. True to their name, gluons "glue" quarks together, forming hadrons such as protons and neutrons.

## Color Charge and Confinement
Unlike photons, which carry no electric charge and do not interact with each other, gluons carry the "color charge" of the strong force. This means gluons not only interact with quarks but also interact with *other gluons*. This self-interaction makes the strong force incredibly complex and leads to "color confinement," meaning quarks and gluons can never be isolated as free particles.

## Examples to Understand Concepts
- **The Sticky Rubber Band:** The strong force is bizarre. Usually, forces get weaker as you pull things apart (like magnets). But pulling quarks apart stretches the gluon field between them like a thick rubber band. The tension increases until the band snaps, creating new quarks at the broken ends.
- **The Mass of a Proton:** The three quarks inside a proton only account for about 1% of the proton's mass. The other 99% comes from the immense kinetic and potential energy of the violently swirling sea of gluons keeping the quarks trapped. The energy of the "glue" is where almost all the mass in your body comes from!

## Recent Research & Discoveries
- High-energy physics experiments are searching for "glueballs," which are hypothetical, extremely unstable composite particles made entirely of gluons, with no quarks at all.
- Simulations using Lattice Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) on the world's most powerful supercomputers are required to calculate the chaotic, self-interacting nature of gluon fields inside the proton.

## Overview
Leptons are a family of elementary particles that, unlike quarks, do not undergo strong interactions. The best known of all leptons is the electron, which governs nearly all of chemistry and electricity. Leptons come in two main classes: charged leptons (like the electron) and neutral leptons (known as neutrinos).

## Three Generations
Like quarks, leptons are grouped into three generations. The first generation consists of the electron and the electron neutrino. The second generation includes the heavier muon and muon neutrino. The third generation consists of the massive tau particle and the tau neutrino. The heavier leptons (muon and tau) are highly unstable and quickly decay into lighter particles.

## Examples to Understand Concepts
- **The Antisocial Particles:** If quarks are highly social particles that always clump together (due to the strong force), leptons are the introverts. They don't feel the strong force, so they happily exist completely alone, flying through space or orbiting nuclei independently.
- **The Heavy Cousins:** Think of the electron as a compact car. The muon is like a heavy truck, and the tau is like a massive freight train. They all do the exact same job and have the same charge, but they are dramatically different in mass.

## Recent Research & Discoveries
- The Muon g-2 experiment at Fermilab recently found strong evidence that muons "wobble" in a magnetic field slightly more than the Standard Model predicts, hinting at undiscovered physics or new particles.
- Deep underground detectors are constantly studying neutrinos to understand their tiny masses and how they "oscillate" between different flavors as they travel.

## Overview
A muon is an elementary particle similar to the electron, with an electric charge of -1 and a spin of 1/2, but with a much greater mass. It is classified as a lepton. Unlike the stable electron, the muon is highly unstable, surviving for only about 2.2 microseconds before decaying into an electron and neutrinos.

## Cosmic Rain
Muons are not generally found in ordinary matter. The vast majority of naturally occurring muons on Earth are created in the upper atmosphere when high-energy cosmic rays (mostly protons from deep space) collide with gas molecules. These collisions create a shower of secondary particles, including muons, which rain down upon the Earth's surface.

## Examples to Understand Concepts
- **The Heavy Battery:** If an electron is a standard AA battery, a muon is an industrial car battery. They carry the exact same charge and do the same job, but the muon is over 200 times heavier.
- **Time Dilation in Action:** Muons decay in just 2.2 microseconds. Moving at the speed of light, they should only travel 660 meters before decaying, meaning none should reach the Earth's surface from the upper atmosphere. However, because they travel so fast, Special Relativity causes time to slow down for them, allowing them to easily reach the ground.

## Recent Research & Discoveries
- The "Muon g-2" experiment has measured the magnetic moment of the muon with unprecedented precision, finding a slight deviation from the Standard Model. This "wobble" suggests there may be undiscovered particles or forces interacting with the muon.
- Muon tomography uses cosmic-ray muons to non-destructively image the interior of massive, dense structures, recently uncovering a hidden void inside the Great Pyramid of Giza.

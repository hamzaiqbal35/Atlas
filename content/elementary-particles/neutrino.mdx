## Overview
A neutrino is a fermion that interacts only via the weak subatomic force and gravity. Because of this, neutrinos typically pass through normal matter unimpeded and undetected. They are incredibly abundant; trillions of neutrinos from the Sun pass through your body every single second.

## Oscillation and Mass
For a long time, neutrinos were thought to be completely massless, like photons. However, physicists discovered that neutrinos "oscillate" or change between three different flavors (electron, muon, and tau neutrinos) as they travel through space. According to quantum mechanics, for them to oscillate and experience time, they *must* have a non-zero mass, though it is so small it has not yet been precisely measured.

## Examples to Understand Concepts
- **The Ghost in the Wall:** If you shine a flashlight at a solid lead wall, the photons are stopped instantly. To a neutrino, a block of lead one light-year thick is mostly transparent. They pass through planets and stars as if they were ghosts.
- **The Chameleon:** Imagine throwing a red ball into the air, and by the time it lands, it has morphed into a blue ball. This is how neutrino oscillation works, constantly shifting identities as they travel through the vacuum of space.

## Recent Research & Discoveries
- Massive underground detectors like Super-Kamiokande in Japan and IceCube in Antarctica use vast tanks of pure water or ice to detect the extremely rare flashes of light caused when a neutrino happens to collide directly with an atom.
- The discovery of neutrino oscillation was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics in 2015. Scientists are now trying to determine if neutrinos are their own antiparticles.

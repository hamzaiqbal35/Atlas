## Overview
A photon is an elementary particle that is a quantum of the electromagnetic field, including electromagnetic radiation such as light and radio waves. It is also the force carrier for the electromagnetic force. Photons are massless, which means they always move at the speed of light in a vacuum.

## The Quantum of Light
Before the 20th century, light was entirely understood as a continuous wave. However, Albert Einstein's work on the photoelectric effect demonstrated that light also behaves as discrete, indivisible packets of energy. These packets are photons. The energy of a photon is directly proportional to its frequency: high-frequency gamma rays carry highly energetic photons, while low-frequency radio waves carry low-energy photons.

## Examples to Understand Concepts
- **The Raindrop Analogy:** Imagine light not as a continuous stream of water from a hose, but as a torrential downpour of individual, tiny raindrops. Each raindrop is a photon. Your eye can detect as few as a handful of these "drops" arriving at once.
- **The Billiard Break:** In the Compton effect, an x-ray photon collides with an electron. The photon bounces off and transfers some of its momentum to the electron, exactly like two billiard balls colliding, proving photons act like physical particles despite having no mass.

## Recent Research & Discoveries
- Technologies involving single-photon emission and detection are at the cutting edge of quantum cryptography, allowing for completely unhackable communication networks.
- Researchers continue to study the interactions of photons in intense electromagnetic fields to probe the quantum vacuum itself.

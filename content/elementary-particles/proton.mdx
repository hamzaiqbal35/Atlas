## Overview
A proton is a subatomic particle with a positive electric charge. While often discussed alongside elementary particles, it is technically a composite particle (a hadron) made of three quarks held together by the strong nuclear force. Protons are found in the nucleus of every atom, and the number of protons in an atom's nucleus dictates its chemical element.

## The Building Block of Elements
The number of protons in a nucleus is called the atomic number. If a nucleus has exactly one proton, it is hydrogen. If it has six, it is carbon. If it has 79, it is gold. This fundamental property makes the proton the defining building block of the periodic table and all of chemistry. 

## Examples to Understand Concepts
- **The Quark Prison:** A proton is not a solid sphere; it is more like a microscopic prison. Inside, two "up" quarks and one "down" quark are violently buzzing around at near the speed of light, locked forever inside by an incredibly strong field of gluons.
- **The Empty Atom:** If an atom were the size of a massive football stadium, the proton at its center would be the size of a single marble resting on the 50-yard line, yet that marble contains almost all of the stadium's mass.

## Recent Research & Discoveries
- The "Proton Radius Puzzle" has been a major subject of modern physics. Different measurement techniques yield slightly different sizes for the proton, leading physicists to question if there is an error in measurement or a flaw in the Standard Model.
- Deep underground experiments, like Super-Kamiokande, continually watch vast tanks of water hoping to see a proton decay, testing Grand Unified Theories. So far, the proton appears to be perfectly stable, with a half-life far exceeding the age of the universe.

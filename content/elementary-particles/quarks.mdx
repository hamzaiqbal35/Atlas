## Overview
Quarks are fundamental constituents of matter. They combine to form composite particles called hadrons, the most stable of which are protons and neutrons, the components of atomic nuclei. Due to a phenomenon known as color confinement, quarks are never found in isolation; they can only be found within hadrons.

## Flavors and Colors
There are six types, known as "flavors", of quarks: up, down, charm, strange, top, and bottom. Up and down quarks have the lowest masses of all quarks and are generally stable and most common in the universe. In addition to flavor, quarks carry a property called "color charge" (red, green, blue), which is how they interact via the strong nuclear force.

## Examples to Understand Concepts
- **The Recipe for a Proton:** A proton is like a tiny recipe that always requires three ingredients: two "up" quarks and one "down" quark. A neutron requires one "up" quark and two "down" quarks.
- **The Rubber Band Effect:** If you try to pull two quarks apart, the strong force between them acts like a rubber band. The harder you pull, the more energy you put into the system. Eventually, the band "snaps," but instead of freeing the quarks, the stored energy creates a brand new pair of quarks.

## Recent Research & Discoveries
- The discovery of the Top quark at Fermilab in 1995 completed the Standard Model's three-generation structure for quarks. Its massive weight (as heavy as a gold atom) remains a subject of intense study.
- Researchers at the LHCb experiment continually discover new states of matter called "tetraquarks" and "pentaquarks," which are exotic hadrons made of four or five quarks bound together.

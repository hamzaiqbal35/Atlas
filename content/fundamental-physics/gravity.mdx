## Overview
Gravity is the fundamental interaction that causes mutual attraction between all things that have mass or energy. Despite being the weakest of the four fundamental forces, it is the dominant force on a macroscopic scale, dictating the orbits of planets, the formation of stars, and the overall structure of the universe.

## The Nature of Gravity
For centuries, gravity was understood through Isaac Newton's law of universal gravitation, which described it as a force acting instantaneously across a distance. However, in 1915, Albert Einstein revolutionized our understanding with his theory of General Relativity. In this framework, gravity is not a traditional force but a consequence of the curvature of spacetime caused by the uneven distribution of mass and energy.

## Examples to Understand Concepts
- **The Rubber Sheet Analogy:** Imagine spacetime as a stretched rubber sheet. A heavy bowling ball (representing a star) placed in the center causes the sheet to dip. If you roll a marble (a planet) across the sheet, it will follow the curve of the dip, orbiting the bowling ball.
- **The Elevator Thought Experiment:** If you were in a closed elevator accelerating upward in deep space at 9.8 m/s², you would feel a downward pull indistinguishable from Earth's gravity. This is the Equivalence Principle, a cornerstone of relativity.

## Recent Research & Discoveries
- Gravitational wave observatories like LIGO and Virgo continue to detect ripples in spacetime from merging black holes and neutron stars.
- Ongoing research aims to reconcile gravity with quantum mechanics, seeking a unified theory of quantum gravity.

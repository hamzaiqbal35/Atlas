## Overview


![Active Galaxy](/active-galaxy-inline.png)


An active galactic nucleus (AGN) is a compact region at the center of a galaxy that has a much-higher-than-normal luminosity over at least some portion of the electromagnetic spectrum.

## Specific Topic Details

A galaxy hosting an AGN is called an "active galaxy". The non-stellar emission from an AGN is theorized to be the result of the accretion of matter by a supermassive black hole at the center of its host galaxy. This encompasses various phenomena, including quasars, blazars, and Seyfert galaxies.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Engine Analogy:** Think of an active galaxy as a vehicle with an oversized, wildly revving engine. The supermassive black hole and its accretion disk form an incredibly powerful central engine that can outshine the rest of the galaxy combined.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- Extensive monitoring campaigns across multiple wavelengths (X-ray, optical, radio) are used to study the rapid variability of AGNs, probing the extreme physics near the black hole.
- Research is focused on the "feedback" mechanism, where the immense energy output from the AGN heats or blows away the galaxy's gas, regulating future star formation.

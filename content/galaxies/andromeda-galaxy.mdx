## Overview

The Andromeda Galaxy, also known as Messier 31, is a barred spiral galaxy approximately 2.5 million light-years from Earth and the nearest major galaxy to the Milky Way.

## Specific Topic Details

Andromeda contains an estimated one trillion stars. Despite having more stars, recent studies suggest its mass might be roughly equal to that of the Milky Way. It is approaching the Milky Way at about 110 kilometers per second and is expected to collide with our galaxy in roughly 4.5 billion years.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Cosmic Dance:** The impending collision between Andromeda and the Milky Way won't be a sudden crash, but a slow, billions-of-years-long gravitational dance that will eventually merge the two into a giant elliptical galaxy, sometimes nicknamed "Milkomeda".

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- Recent studies using the Hubble Space Telescope have mapped the massive halo of gas surrounding Andromeda, finding it extends much further than previously thought.
- Simulations of the future Milky Way-Andromeda collision are continually refined, suggesting our Solar System will likely be flung into a new orbit.

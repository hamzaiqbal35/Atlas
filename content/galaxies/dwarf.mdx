## Overview

A dwarf galaxy is a small galaxy composed of up to several billion stars, a small number compared to our Milky Way's 200–400 billion stars.

## Specific Topic Details

The Large Magellanic Cloud, which closely orbits the Milky Way, is an example. Dwarf galaxies are the most abundant type of galaxy in the universe but are hard to detect due to their low luminosity. They are often found in clusters, orbiting larger host galaxies, and are believed to be heavily dominated by dark matter.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Satellite Analogy:** Just as moons orbit planets, dwarf galaxies act as galactic satellites, orbiting larger, more massive galaxies like our Milky Way.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- Dwarf galaxies are crucial for testing models of dark matter, as they seem to have a much higher ratio of dark matter to normal matter than larger galaxies.
- Astronomers are constantly discovering new, extremely faint dwarf galaxies in our Local Group.

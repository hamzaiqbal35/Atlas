## Overview

Elliptical galaxies are a type of galaxy having an approximately ellipsoidal shape and a smooth, nearly featureless image. They are one of the three main classes of galaxy described by Edwin Hubble.

## Specific Topic Details

These galaxies range in shape from nearly spherical to highly elongated. They are dominated by older stellar populations, resulting in a reddish-yellow color. Unlike spiral galaxies, ellipticals lack significant amounts of cold gas and dust, meaning very little new star formation occurs within them. They are often found near the centers of galaxy clusters.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Swarm of Bees Analogy:** Instead of moving in an organized, rotating disk, the stars in an elliptical galaxy move in more random, chaotic orbits, like a swarm of bees buzzing around a central point.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- Astronomers study the subtle motions of stars in ellipticals to map the distribution of dark matter within them.
- Research continues into how ellipticals form, with the leading theory being that they are the result of major mergers between other types of galaxies.

## Overview


![Lenticular Galaxy](/lenticular-inline.png)


Lenticular galaxies are a type of galaxy intermediate between an elliptical and a spiral galaxy. They contain a large-scale disk but do not have large-scale spiral structure.

## Specific Topic Details

Like ellipticals, lenticulars have lost or used up most of their interstellar matter and therefore have very little ongoing star formation. They consist mainly of aging stars. The presence of a prominent disk distinguishes them from ellipticals, suggesting they may have once been spiral galaxies that lost their gas.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Faded Photograph Analogy:** A lenticular galaxy is like a faded photograph of a spiral galaxy. The basic shape (the disk) is still there, but the bright, defining details (the spiral arms of new stars) have faded away.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- The exact origins of lenticulars are debated; some may be "faded" spirals, while others might be the result of galactic mergers.
- Studies of their globular cluster populations are helping to unravel their complex evolutionary histories.

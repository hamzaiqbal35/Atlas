## Overview

The Milky Way is the galaxy that contains our Solar System, with the name describing the galaxy's appearance from Earth: a hazy band of light seen in the night sky formed from stars that cannot be individually distinguished by the naked eye. It is a large barred spiral galaxy.

## Specific Topic Details

The Milky Way consists of a bar-shaped core region surrounded by a warped disk of gas, dust and stars. The mass of the Milky Way is estimated at about 1.5 trillion solar masses, and it contains between 100 and 400 billion stars. At its very center lies Sagittarius A*, a supermassive black hole with a mass of 4.14 million suns.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Frisbee Analogy:** Imagine a spinning frisbee with a slightly thicker center. The flat part is where most stars (including our Sun) orbit, while the thick center represents the galactic bulge.
- **The Traffic Jam Analogy:** Spiral arms aren't fixed structures of stars; they are like cosmic traffic jams. Stars and gas move into the spiral arm, slow down due to density, and then exit on the other side.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- Data from the Gaia space observatory is providing an unprecedented 3D map of over a billion stars in the Milky Way, revealing past collisions with other smaller galaxies.
- The Event Horizon Telescope recently captured the first direct image of Sagittarius A*, confirming the existence of the supermassive black hole at our galactic center.

## Overview

Radio galaxies and their relatives, radio-loud quasars and blazars, are types of active galaxies that are very luminous at radio wavelengths.

## Specific Topic Details

The radio emission is due to the synchrotron process. The observed structure in radio emission is determined by the interaction between twin jets of highly relativistic plasma, ejected from an active galactic nucleus (powered by a supermassive black hole), and the external medium. They are typically found in large elliptical galaxies.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Lighthouse Analogy:** The supermassive black hole at the center acts like the powerful bulb of a lighthouse, while the magnetic fields channel the energy into tight beams (jets) that shine brightly across the cosmos.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- Radio telescope arrays like the VLA and LOFAR are mapping the extended lobes of radio galaxies, which can stretch for millions of light-years into intergalactic space.
- Astronomers study these jets to understand how supermassive black holes affect the evolution of their host galaxies and surrounding clusters.

## Overview

Spiral galaxies form a class of galaxy characterized by a flat, rotating disk containing stars, gas and dust, and a central concentration of stars known as the bulge.

## Specific Topic Details

These are often surrounded by a much fainter halo of stars. The spiral arms are regions of higher density that trigger star formation as gas and dust clouds move through them, resulting in bright, blue young stars that make the arms stand out. They make up a large fraction of all the galaxies in the local universe.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Sprinkler Analogy:** A rotating lawn sprinkler creates curved streams of water. Similarly, the rotating disk of a spiral galaxy, combined with density waves, creates the sweeping spiral arms.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- Studies using ALMA (Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array) are tracing the cold molecular gas to understand how spiral arms drive star formation.
- The rotation curves of spiral galaxies remain one of the strongest pieces of evidence for the existence of dark matter.

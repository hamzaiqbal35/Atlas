## Overview


![Starburst Core](/starburst-core.png)


A starburst galaxy is a galaxy undergoing an exceptionally high rate of star formation, compared to the long-term average rate of star formation in the galaxy or the star formation rate observed in most other galaxies.

## Specific Topic Details

If a starburst galaxy continued forming stars at its current rate, its gas reservoir would be depleted in a timeframe much shorter than the age of the galaxy. This means the starburst phase is a temporary, relatively short-lived period in a galaxy's evolution. They are often triggered by galactic mergers or close encounters.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Fireworks Analogy:** A starburst galaxy is like a grand finale of a fireworks show. Instead of a steady, occasional pop, all the combustible material is being used up in a massive, bright, and relatively brief display.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- Infrared observatories like JWST are ideal for studying starbursts, as the dense dust clouds where stars form absorb visible light and re-emit it in the infrared.
- Studying local starburst galaxies helps astronomers understand the intense star-forming environments common in the early universe.

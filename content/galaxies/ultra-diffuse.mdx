## Overview


![Ultra Diffuse Galaxy](/ultra-diffuse-inline.png)


An ultra-diffuse galaxy (UDG) is an extremely low-density galaxy. They can be the size of the Milky Way but have only 1% as many stars.

## Specific Topic Details

Because they have so few stars spread out over such a large volume, their surface brightness is extremely low, making them very difficult to detect. The origin of UDGs remains a mystery. Some might be "failed" galaxies that lost their gas early on, while others might be dwarf galaxies that were puffed up by internal or external forces.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Cotton Candy Analogy:** An ultra-diffuse galaxy is like a piece of cotton candy. It takes up a lot of space and looks large, but there is very little actual substance to it compared to a dense candy bar (a normal galaxy).

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- Recent discoveries of UDGs that seem to lack dark matter entirely have challenged current theories of galaxy formation.
- Deep imaging surveys continue to reveal large populations of UDGs in galaxy clusters like the Coma Cluster.

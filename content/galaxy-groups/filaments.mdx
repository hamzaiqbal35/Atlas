## Overview

Galaxy filaments are the largest known structures in the universe. They are massive, thread-like formations that form the boundaries between large voids.
## Specific Topic Details

These structures consist of gravitationally bound galaxies, gas, and dark matter. They intersect at dense nodes where galaxy clusters and superclusters are found. Filaments make up the complex, branching structure known as the "Cosmic Web", which defines the large-scale structure of the observable universe.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Spiderweb Analogy:** The universe on its largest scale looks incredibly like a spiderweb. The silk threads are the filaments of dark matter and galaxies, and the dewdrops caught where the threads cross are the massive galaxy clusters.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- Recent observations using the "intergalactic medium absorption" technique have directly detected the faint, hot gas that makes up the glowing threads of the filaments.
- Cosmological simulations aim to understand how these filaments formed from quantum fluctuations in the early universe.

## Overview

Galaxy clusters are the largest known gravitationally bound structures in the universe, consisting of hundreds to thousands of galaxies.

![Galaxy Clusters](/galaxy-clusters-inline.png)

## Specific Topic Details

They are held together by the immense gravity of dark matter. Only about 1% of a cluster's mass is locked up in stars and galaxies; another 9% is in the form of a million-degree intergalactic gas known as the intracluster medium (ICM), which emits strongly in X-rays. The remaining 90% is invisible dark matter.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Swarm Analogy:** A galaxy cluster is like a massive swarm of glowing bees (galaxies) trapped in an invisible jar (dark matter), surrounded by a thin, scalding hot mist (the ICM).

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- X-ray observatories like Chandra study the hot intracluster gas to measure the total mass of the cluster, including dark matter.
- Strong gravitational lensing around dense clusters is used as a natural "magnifying glass" to observe extremely distant background galaxies.

## Overview

Superclusters are massive groups of smaller galaxy clusters or galaxy groups. They are among the largest known structures of the cosmos.

![Superclusters](/superclusters-core.png)

## Specific Topic Details

Unlike clusters, most superclusters are not gravitationally bound; the clusters within them are drifting apart due to the expansion of the universe. They form massive, branching, filamentary structures that look like glowing webs. The Milky Way belongs to the Local Group, which is part of the Virgo Supercluster, which is a component of the even larger Laniakea Supercluster.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Highway Network Analogy:** If galaxy clusters are major bustling cities, superclusters are the sprawling networks of interstate highways and suburbs that connect these cities together across entire continents.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- Astronomers use the peculiar velocities (movement independent of cosmic expansion) of thousands of galaxies to map the invisible gravitational "watersheds" that define superclusters.
- The exact boundaries of superclusters like Laniakea are constantly being refined through massive 3D mapping surveys.

## Overview

Cosmic voids are vast, seemingly empty spaces between galactic filaments, the largest-scale structures in the universe.

![Cosmic Voids](/voids-inline.png)

## Specific Topic Details

They contain very few, or no, galaxies. Voids typically have a diameter of 30 to 300 million light-years. While they appear empty, they are thought to contain dark matter and dark energy. The expansion of the universe is causing these voids to grow larger over time, pushing the surrounding filaments and clusters closer together.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Swiss Cheese Analogy:** The large-scale structure of the universe is often compared to Swiss cheese. The solid cheese represents the filaments and clusters of galaxies, while the large, empty holes represent the cosmic voids.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- Astronomers study the very few, isolated galaxies (void galaxies) found within these regions to understand how galaxies evolve without the gravitational influence or collisions common in dense clusters.
- The accelerating expansion of these voids is a key area of study for understanding the nature of Dark Energy.

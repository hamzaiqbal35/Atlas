## Overview

A galactic bulge is a tightly packed group of stars within a larger star formation. The term almost exclusively refers to the central group of stars found in most spiral galaxies.

![Galactic Bulge](/bulge-inline.png)

## Specific Topic Details

Bulges are generally composed of older, redder stars (Population II stars) and contain very little gas and dust, meaning there is minimal new star formation. They are thought to be formed either by the collapse of a primordial gas cloud or through the merging of smaller galaxies early in the universe's history. In barred spirals like ours, the bulge can have a peanut or boxy shape.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Yolk of an Egg:** If a spiral galaxy is a fried egg, the bulge is the yolk—a distinct, dense, and older central component protruding from the flatter disk (the egg white).

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- Recent surveys suggest the Milky Way's bulge is X-shaped, a feature commonly associated with the buckling of a central bar.
- Spectroscopic analysis of bulge stars helps trace the chemical enrichment history of the early galaxy.

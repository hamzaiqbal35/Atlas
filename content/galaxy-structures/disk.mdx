## Overview

The galactic disk is the flattened, circular region of a spiral or lenticular galaxy where the majority of its young stars, gas, and dust are located.

![Galactic Disk](/disk-inline.png)

## Specific Topic Details

Because it is rich in cold gas and molecular clouds, the disk is the primary site of active star formation. It rotates differentially, meaning the inner parts rotate faster than the outer parts. When viewed edge-on, the disk often features a prominent dark lane of dust that obscures the starlight behind it. Our Solar System resides within the disk of the Milky Way.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Vinyl Record:** The galactic disk is much like a vinyl record—flat, circular, and rotating, containing the "grooves" (spiral arms) where the most active and noticeable features reside.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- Radio astronomy is used to map the atomic hydrogen gas in the disk, revealing its warped outer edges.
- Astronomers study the vertical structure of the disk (the "thin" and "thick" disks) to understand the heating mechanisms that scatter stars over billions of years.

## Overview

The galactic core, or galactic center, is the rotational center of a galaxy. It is an extreme environment characterized by an incredibly high density of stars, intense radiation, and typically, a supermassive black hole.
## Specific Topic Details

In our Milky Way, the galactic core is located in the direction of the constellation Sagittarius. The stars in this region are packed so tightly that if we lived there, the night sky would be blindingly bright. The supermassive black hole at the center (Sagittarius A*) dictates the dynamics of the innermost stars, whipping them around at fractions of the speed of light.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Eye of the Hurricane:** The galactic core is like the eye of a cosmic storm. It is the dense, intense center around which the entire massive structure of the galaxy slowly rotates.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- The Event Horizon Telescope continues to monitor Sagittarius A* to understand how matter falls into the supermassive black hole.
- Astronomers track the orbits of individual stars (like the S2 star) near the core to test Einstein's Theory of General Relativity in extreme gravitational fields.

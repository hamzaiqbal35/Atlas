## Overview

The stellar halo is a spherical, extended, and roughly diffuse region of a galaxy that surrounds the galactic disk and bulge. It contains the oldest stars in the galaxy.
## Specific Topic Details

The halo is home to globular clusters—tight, spherical collections of hundreds of thousands of ancient stars. The stars in the halo are metal-poor, meaning they formed early in the universe before many generations of supernovae could seed the cosmos with heavier elements. The stellar halo extends far beyond the visible disk of the galaxy.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Halo of a Streetlamp:** Just as a streetlamp on a foggy night has a faint, spherical glow extending far beyond the bright bulb and fixture, a galaxy has a faint spherical halo of stars extending far beyond its bright disk.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- The Gaia mission is mapping streams of stars in the Milky Way's halo, which are the shredded remnants of dwarf galaxies consumed by our galaxy in the past.
- Studying halo stars provides a "fossil record" of the earliest stages of galactic assembly.

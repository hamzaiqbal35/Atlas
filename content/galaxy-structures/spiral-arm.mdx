## Overview

Spiral arms are regions of stars that extend from the center of spiral and barred spiral galaxies. They resemble the arms of a pinwheel.
## Specific Topic Details

Contrary to appearances, spiral arms are not solid, rigid structures of stars rotating together. The leading theory is the "Density Wave Theory", which posits that the arms are areas of greater density moving through the galactic disk, similar to a traffic jam. As gas clouds enter this dense region, they are compressed, triggering the collapse of the clouds and the birth of massive, bright blue stars that illuminate the arm.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Traffic Jam Analogy:** Cars (stars and gas) move into a congested area (the spiral arm), slow down, and then exit. The traffic jam itself persists, even though the individual cars within it are constantly changing.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- High-resolution infrared imaging by JWST is peering through the dust of spiral arms to see the very earliest stages of star formation.
- Astronomers map the distribution of molecular clouds (using carbon monoxide emissions) to trace the exact structure and spacing of the arms.

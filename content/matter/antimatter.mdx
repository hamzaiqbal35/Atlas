## Overview
Antimatter is material composed of the antiparticle partners to the corresponding particles of ordinary matter. Every type of fundamental particle has a corresponding antiparticle with the same mass but opposite physical charges (such as electric charge). For example, the antiparticle of an electron is a positively charged positron.

## Matter-Antimatter Annihilation
When a particle and its antiparticle collide, they annihilate each other, completely destroying both particles and converting their entire mass into pure energy, typically in the form of high-energy gamma rays. This process is 100% efficient at converting mass to energy, governed by Einstein's famous equation, E=mc².

## Examples to Understand Concepts
- **The Mirror Universe:** Imagine a universe exactly like ours, but every positive charge is negative and every negative charge is positive. Chemistry and physics would work exactly the same way, but if our universe touched the mirror universe, both would be destroyed in a flash of light.
- **PET Scans:** Antimatter isn't just science fiction. Positron Emission Tomography (PET) scans in medicine actively use radioactive tracers that emit positrons (anti-electrons) to detect diseases like cancer in the human body.

## Recent Research & Discoveries
- The ALPHA experiment at CERN successfully trapped antihydrogen atoms and measured their optical spectrum, confirming they emit the exact same frequencies of light as regular hydrogen.
- One of the biggest unsolved mysteries in physics is Baryogenesis: why is there so much normal matter in the universe and almost no antimatter, when the Big Bang should have created equal amounts of both?

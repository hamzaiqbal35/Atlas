## Overview
Dark energy is an unknown form of energy that affects the universe on the largest scales. The primary effect of dark energy is that it acts as a repulsive force, accelerating the expansion of the universe. It is the most dominant component of the cosmos, making up roughly 68% of the total mass-energy of the universe.

## The Accelerating Universe
For decades, cosmologists assumed the expansion of the universe (started by the Big Bang) was slowing down due to the inward pull of gravity. However, in 1998, observations of distant Type Ia supernovae revealed that the expansion is actually speeding up. Dark energy was proposed to explain this anti-gravity effect.

## Examples to Understand Concepts
- **The Raisin Bread Analogy:** Imagine baking a loaf of raisin bread. As the dough rises and expands, the raisins (galaxies) move farther apart. Dark energy is like the yeast in the dough, but instead of slowing down, the rising process is accelerating indefinitely.
- **The Cosmological Constant:** Albert Einstein originally introduced a "cosmological constant" into his equations of General Relativity to keep the universe static. He later called it his "biggest blunder," but today, it is the leading mathematical explanation for dark energy.

## Recent Research & Discoveries
- The Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) is currently mapping millions of galaxies and quasars to create the most detailed 3D map of the universe, aiming to measure the expansion history and the nature of dark energy with unprecedented precision.
- Theories of "quintessence" suggest dark energy might not be a constant, but a dynamic, evolving scalar field that changes over cosmic time.

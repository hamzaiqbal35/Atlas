## Overview
Normal matter, also known as baryonic matter, is the everyday material we interact with. It comprises everything we can see, touch, and directly measure—from the device you're reading this on to the farthest visible galaxies. Surprisingly, despite being everything we intimately know, it makes up less than 5% of the total mass-energy budget of the universe.

## Composition and Structure
At its core, normal matter is built from atoms. These atoms consist of a dense nucleus made of protons and neutrons (which are themselves made of quarks), surrounded by a cloud of much lighter electrons. The way these atoms bond and interact gives rise to chemistry and the vast complexity of the macroscopic world.

## Examples to Understand Concepts
- **The Four States:** Depending on temperature and pressure, normal matter exists in different states. Ice (solid) melts into water (liquid), boils into steam (gas), and if heated intensely enough, its electrons are stripped away to form plasma (like the Sun).
- **The Baryonic Census:** When astronomers say "baryonic matter," they mean matter made of baryons (protons and neutrons). Though electrons are leptons, their mass is so small that baryons account for over 99.9% of the mass of normal matter.

## Recent Research & Discoveries
- Astronomers recently solved the "missing baryon problem," successfully locating the vast clouds of diffuse, highly ionized gas (the Warm-Hot Intergalactic Medium) that bridge galaxies and account for the remaining expected normal matter in the universe.

## Overview

A black dwarf is a theoretical stellar remnant, specifically a white dwarf that has cooled sufficiently that it no longer emits significant heat or light.
## Specific Topic Details

Because the time required for a white dwarf to reach this state is calculated to be longer than the current age of the universe (13.8 billion years), no black dwarfs are expected to exist in the universe currently. When they eventually do form, they will be cold, dense, invisible spheres of degenerate carbon and oxygen.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Frozen Ash Analogy:** If a white dwarf is the glowing ember left after a fire, a black dwarf is that same ember trillions of years later—completely cold, frozen, and indistinguishable from the dark ground around it.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- While unobservable, theoretical physics predicts their eventual fate. In the extremely distant future (10^1500 years), quantum tunneling might cause them to collapse into black holes or slowly undergo iron-56 fusion.

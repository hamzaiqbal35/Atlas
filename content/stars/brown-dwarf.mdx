## Overview

Brown dwarfs are substellar objects that are not massive enough to sustain nuclear fusion of ordinary hydrogen into helium in their cores, unlike a main-sequence star.

![Brown Dwarf](/brown-dwarf-inline.png)

## Specific Topic Details

They occupy the mass range between the heaviest gas giant planets and the lightest stars. While they cannot fuse ordinary hydrogen, those above 13 Jupiter masses can temporarily fuse deuterium, and the most massive ones can fuse lithium. They glow dimly in the infrared due to the heat generated during their initial contraction.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Smoldering Charcoal Analogy:** A star is a blazing fire. A planet is a cold rock. A brown dwarf is a piece of charcoal that got warm but never quite caught on fire—it glows faintly with residual heat but doesn't have an active flame.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- Observations are focused on their complex atmospheres, which are cool enough to form clouds of silicate dust, iron, and salts, blurring the line between star and planet.
- Astronomers debate the exact formation mechanism of brown dwarfs: do they form like stars (collapsing clouds) or like planets (in a disk around a star)?

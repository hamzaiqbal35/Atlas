## Overview

Hypergiants are stars with enormous mass and luminosity, showing signs of extremely high rates of mass loss.
## Specific Topic Details

They are pushing the Eddington limit—the point at which a star is so luminous that the outward pressure of its own radiation starts to blow its outer layers away into space. Because of this, they are highly unstable and undergo massive eruptions. VY Canis Majoris is a famous example of a red hypergiant.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Overfilled Balloon Analogy:** A hypergiant is like a balloon that has been filled with so much air (energy) that the rubber is stretched to its absolute breaking point, constantly leaking and on the verge of violently bursting.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- Luminous Blue Variables (LBVs), a type of hypergiant, are studied to understand the mechanisms behind their massive, sporadic eruptions.
- The upper mass limit of stars (around 150-300 solar masses) is actively debated and investigated by observing hypergiants in the Magellanic Clouds.

## Overview

A main sequence star is any star that is fusing hydrogen in its core and has a stable balance of outward pressure from core nuclear fusion and gravitational forces pushing inward.

![Main Sequence](/main-sequence-inline.png)

## Specific Topic Details

This state of balance is called hydrostatic equilibrium. Stars spend about 90% of their active lifetimes on the main sequence. The mass of the star determines its temperature, luminosity, and how long it will remain on the main sequence. Massive stars burn their fuel quickly, while low-mass stars can burn steadily for trillions of years.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Tug-of-War Analogy:** The star is in a constant, perfect tug-of-war. Gravity is pulling everything inward, trying to crush the star, while the explosive energy of nuclear fusion is pushing outward. On the main sequence, neither side wins.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- Asteroseismology is used to study the internal structure and rotation of main sequence stars by observing their pulsations.
- Surveys aim to understand the "initial mass function"—the exact proportion of high-mass to low-mass stars created in a typical nebula.

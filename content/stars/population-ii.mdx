## Overview

Population II stars are older, less luminous, and cooler than Population I stars. They have very low "metallicity", meaning they are almost entirely hydrogen and helium.

![Population II](/population-ii-inline.png)

## Specific Topic Details

They formed early in the universe's history, before many heavy elements had been created and dispersed by supernovae. In our galaxy, they are primarily found in the stellar halo, the galactic bulge, and within ancient globular clusters. They represent a fossil record of the early Milky Way.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Historic Ruins Analogy:** Population II stars are like ancient stone ruins. They are built from the simplest, most basic materials available at the dawn of civilization (the early universe), long before complex modern materials were invented.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- Identifying the most metal-poor Population II stars ("ultra metal-poor stars") provides clues about the nature of the very first stars to ever exist.
- Their kinematic properties (how they move) help astronomers understand how the Milky Way originally assembled from smaller dwarf galaxies.

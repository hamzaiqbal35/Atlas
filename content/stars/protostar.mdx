## Overview

A protostar is a very young star that is still gathering mass from its parent molecular cloud. The protostellar phase is the earliest one in the process of stellar evolution.
## Specific Topic Details

During this phase, the star is not yet hot enough in its core to begin nuclear fusion. It shines due to the release of gravitational potential energy as it collapses. This stage lasts for about 500,000 years for a star like our Sun.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Snowball Analogy:** Imagine rolling a snowball down a snowy hill. It gathers more and more snow, growing denser and heavier. A protostar is gathering gas and dust from its surroundings until it becomes massive and hot enough to ignite.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- JWST is peering through the dense dust of stellar nurseries to observe protostars directly in the infrared.
- Observations of bipolar outflows (jets of gas) help scientists understand how protostars shed angular momentum as they collapse.

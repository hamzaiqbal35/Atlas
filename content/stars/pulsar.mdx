## Overview

A pulsar is a highly magnetized rotating neutron star that emits beams of electromagnetic radiation out of its magnetic poles.
## Specific Topic Details

This radiation can be observed only when a beam of emission is pointing toward Earth, much like the way a lighthouse can be seen only when the light is pointed in the direction of an observer. The rotation is incredibly stable, meaning the "pulses" arrive with the precision of an atomic clock. Millisecond pulsars can spin hundreds of times every second.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Lighthouse Analogy:** The pulsar acts exactly like a cosmic lighthouse. The star rotates continuously, but we only see a "flash" when the beam of radiation happens to sweep directly across our line of sight.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- Pulsar Timing Arrays (PTAs) monitor a network of pulsars across the galaxy to detect the subtle, long-wavelength gravitational waves passing through our neighborhood from merging supermassive black holes.
- Pulsars in binary systems are used as extreme laboratories to test the limits of General Relativity.

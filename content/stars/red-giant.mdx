## Overview

A red giant is a luminous giant star of low or intermediate mass in a late phase of stellar evolution. The outer atmosphere is inflated and tenuous, making the radius large and the surface temperature low.
## Specific Topic Details

When a main sequence star exhausts the hydrogen in its core, fusion stops, and the core contracts and heats up. This ignites a shell of hydrogen around the core, causing the outer layers of the star to expand massively. When our Sun becomes a red giant in about 5 billion years, it will likely swallow Mercury, Venus, and possibly Earth.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Hot Air Balloon Analogy:** As the burner (the core/shell) gets hotter, the balloon (the star's outer layers) expands massively, cooling down at its outermost edges while taking up vastly more space.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- Astronomers observe the winds of red giants to understand how they enrich the interstellar medium with carbon and oxygen.
- Studies focus on how surviving planetary systems are affected by a star's red giant phase.

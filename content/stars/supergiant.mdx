## Overview

Supergiants are among the most massive and most luminous stars. They occupy the top region of the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram.

![Supergiant](/supergiant-inline.png)

## Specific Topic Details

Supergiants can be red (like Betelgeuse) or blue (like Rigel). Red supergiants have the largest radii, sometimes large enough to encompass the orbit of Jupiter if placed in our Solar System. Blue supergiants are much hotter and more compact but extremely luminous. They have complex interiors with layers fusing different elements up to iron.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Onion Analogy:** A late-stage supergiant is like an onion. It has distinct layers fusing different elements: hydrogen on the outside, then helium, carbon, oxygen, neon, silicon, all the way down to a core of iron.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- Astronomers closely monitor nearby red supergiants like Betelgeuse for variations in brightness that might precede a supernova.
- Models of mass loss via stellar winds are crucial for understanding the final masses of supergiants before they explode.

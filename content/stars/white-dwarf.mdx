## Overview

A white dwarf is a stellar core remnant composed mostly of electron-degenerate matter. It is what stars like our Sun become after they have exhausted their nuclear fuel.
## Specific Topic Details

After shedding its outer layers as a planetary nebula, the core collapses until quantum mechanical effects (electron degeneracy pressure) halt further collapse. A white dwarf packs the mass of a star into a volume the size of the Earth, making it incredibly dense. With no fusion occurring, they slowly radiate away their residual heat over billions of years.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Dying Ember Analogy:** After a roaring campfire (a main-sequence star) burns out and its smoke blows away (a planetary nebula), what remains is a glowing, intensely hot ember (the white dwarf) that slowly cools over a very long time.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- White dwarfs in binary systems can accumulate mass from a companion and explode as Type Ia supernovae, which are crucial "standard candles" for measuring the expansion of the universe.
- Cooling models of white dwarfs are used as cosmic clocks to date the ages of star clusters and the galactic disk.

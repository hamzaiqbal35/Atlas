## Overview

Wolf-Rayet (WR) stars are a rare, heterogeneous set of stars with unusual spectra showing prominent broad emission lines of ionized helium and highly ionized nitrogen or carbon.

![Wolf-Rayet Star](/wolf-rayet-inline.png)

## Specific Topic Details

These lines indicate very high surface temperatures and strong, continuous, and high-velocity stellar winds. They are essentially massive stars that have completely blown away their outer hydrogen envelopes, exposing their blazing hot, helium-fusing cores to space. They are the immediate progenitors of certain types of supernovae.

## Examples to Understand Concepts

- **The Sandblaster Analogy:** A Wolf-Rayet star has stellar winds so powerful they act like a cosmic sandblaster, violently stripping away the star's own outer layers at speeds of millions of miles per hour.

## Recent Research & Discoveries

- WR stars are prime candidates for the progenitors of long-duration Gamma-Ray Bursts (GRBs) if they are rotating rapidly enough when they collapse.
- Their intense winds are studied for the massive kinetic energy they inject into the interstellar medium, shaping the surrounding galaxy.

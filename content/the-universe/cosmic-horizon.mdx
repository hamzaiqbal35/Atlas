## Overview

The cosmic horizon (often referring specifically to the **particle horizon**) is the maximum distance from which particles—including light photons—could have traveled to the observer in the entire age of the universe. It represents the absolute boundary between the observable and the unobservable regions of the cosmos.

Because the universe is 13.8 billion years old, light from objects beyond this horizon simply hasn't had enough time to reach Earth. These objects are completely hidden from our view, rendering them mathematically unobservable regardless of the power of our telescopes.

## Horizon Definitions

Astrophysicists distinguish between several different types of cosmological horizons, each with distinct physical implications:

### 1. The Particle Horizon
The maximum distance from which light from the past can reach us *today*. As time passes, light from increasingly distant regions has time to reach us, meaning the particle horizon is constantly expanding and encompassing new galaxies. Its current radius is ~46.5 billion light-years.

### 2. The Cosmic Event Horizon
The maximum distance from which light emitted *right now* can ever reach us in the *future*. 
Due to the accelerating expansion of the universe (driven by dark energy), galaxies currently beyond our event horizon are receding from us faster than the speed of light. Any light they emit today will never reach us. Its current radius is ~16 billion light-years.

### 3. The Hubble Horizon
The distance at which the recession velocity of galaxies exactly equals the speed of light. Objects beyond the Hubble horizon are currently moving away from us faster than light, though their past light can still reach us.

## The Future of the Horizon

Because the expansion of the universe is accelerating, our cosmic event horizon is actually shrinking in terms of comoving distance. 

Eventually, as the expansion continues to accelerate, more and more galaxies will cross the event horizon, fading from view. Trillions of years from now, the universe will expand so rapidly that all galaxies outside our Local Group will become unobservable, leaving our descendants in a vast, dark, and seemingly empty cosmos.

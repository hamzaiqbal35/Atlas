## Overview

The "Dark Universe" refers to the overwhelming majority of the cosmos that is entirely invisible to human eyes and electromagnetic instruments. Everything we can see, touch, and directly interact with—all the stars, planets, nebulas, and galaxies composed of ordinary baryonic matter—makes up less than **5%** of the universe's total energy budget.

The remaining 95% is composed of **dark matter** and **dark energy**, two profoundly mysterious components that we cannot directly observe. Their existence is inferred entirely from their massive gravitational effects on the visible universe.

## Dark Matter (26.8%)

Dark matter is a hypothetical form of matter that does not emit, absorb, or reflect electromagnetic radiation (light), making it perfectly transparent and invisible.

### Evidence for Dark Matter
1. **Galactic Rotation Curves:** Galaxies rotate much faster than they should based solely on the visible matter they contain. Without a massive, invisible halo of dark matter providing extra gravitational glue, spinning galaxies would fly apart.
2. **Gravitational Lensing:** Astronomers observe the light from distant background galaxies being heavily distorted and magnified by foreground clusters. The amount of distortion requires vastly more mass than the visible galaxies in the cluster can provide.
3. **Cosmic Structure:** Dark matter acted as the foundational scaffolding of the early universe, drawing ordinary matter into its gravitational wells to form the cosmic web.

While we know it exists, its composition remains unknown. Leading candidates include WIMPs (Weakly Interacting Massive Particles) and axions.

## Dark Energy (68.3%)

While dark matter uses gravity to pull the universe together, **dark energy** exerts a negative pressure that pushes the universe apart. It is an enigmatic form of vacuum energy that smoothly permeates all of space and is responsible for accelerating the expansion of the universe.

### Theoretical Models
- **Cosmological Constant ($\Lambda$):** Proposed by Einstein, this model suggests dark energy is the intrinsic energy of space itself. As space expands, more space is created, leading to more dark energy and faster expansion.
- **Quintessence:** A dynamic, time-evolving, and spatially dependent field that differs from a constant vacuum energy.

Understanding the true nature of the dark universe remains the premier challenge in modern astrophysics, requiring new physics beyond the Standard Model.

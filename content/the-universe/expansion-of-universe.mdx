## Overview

The expansion of the universe is the fundamental increase in distance between gravitationally unbound parts of the observable universe over time. It is an intrinsic expansion whereby the scale of space itself changes. 

Crucially, galaxies are not simply moving away from each other *through* space like shrapnel from an explosion; **space itself is expanding**, carrying the galaxies with it like raisins in a rising loaf of bread or dots drawn on an expanding balloon.

## Historical Discovery

In 1929, astronomer **Edwin Hubble** made a paradigm-shifting discovery. By observing the redshift of light from distant galaxies, he realized that virtually all galaxies were moving away from the Milky Way. Furthermore, the farther away a galaxy was, the faster it appeared to be receding. 

This relationship, known as **Hubble's Law** ($v = H_0D$), provided the first observational evidence for an expanding universe and laid the groundwork for the Big Bang theory. The rate of this expansion is measured by the **Hubble Constant**.

## The Shock of Acceleration

For decades, cosmologists assumed that the collective gravitational pull of all the matter in the universe would act as a brake, gradually slowing down the expansion over billions of years. 

However, in 1998, two independent teams of astronomers measuring the distances to distant Type Ia supernovae revealed a shocking truth: **the expansion of the universe is not slowing down; it is accelerating.** The galaxies are flying apart faster and faster as time goes on.

## The Engine of Expansion: Dark Energy

The mysterious force driving this accelerated expansion was dubbed "dark energy." While its exact physical nature remains one of the greatest unsolved problems in modern physics, dark energy appears to act as a repulsive force that permeates all of empty space.

Unlike matter, which dilutes as the universe expands, the density of dark energy remains constant. Therefore, as the universe grows larger and more empty space is created, the total amount of dark energy increases, driving an ever-faster acceleration. Currently, dark energy is estimated to account for roughly **68.3%** of the total mass-energy density of the universe, dictating its ultimate, likely freezing, fate.
